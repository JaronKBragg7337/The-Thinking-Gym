# Impossible Problems

## Purpose

Explore problems with no obvious, complete, or currently achievable solution. The exercise trains first-principles reasoning, constraint analysis, reframing, synthesis, and productive work under uncertainty.

## Core method

Choose a difficult problem, define what “solved” would mean, identify hard constraints, and generate multiple fundamentally different approaches. Do not assume the first framing is correct.

## Rules

1. Define the problem and success criteria precisely.
2. Separate physical impossibility, current technical limitation, economic difficulty, political resistance, and conceptual confusion.
3. Identify which constraints are fixed and which are merely conventional.
4. Generate at least two substantially different solution paths.
5. Examine second-order effects and new problems created by each path.
6. Permit partial solutions, staged progress, and reframing when total resolution is unavailable.
7. State what evidence or breakthrough would alter the conclusion.

## Completion standard

The artifact contains a constraint map, multiple solution architectures, tradeoffs, failure points, and the most promising next question or experiment.

# Convince Me

## Purpose

Convince Me trains belief testing, steelmanning, confidence calibration, and the separation of identity from conclusion.

The participant offers a belief they genuinely hold. The opposing side constructs the strongest honest case that the belief is wrong, incomplete, or framed too broadly. The participant then answers that case. The session ends by determining what survived and what changed.

## Skills trained

- intellectual flexibility;
- adversarial reasoning;
- charitable interpretation;
- assumption detection;
- confidence calibration; and
- precise revision of beliefs.

## Rules

1. Select a belief that matters. Do not choose a disposable position merely to make the exercise easy.
2. Record the belief and confidence before the challenge begins.
3. Define important terms before arguing when ambiguity could decide the outcome.
4. Build the strongest available opposing case. Do not rely on straw men, insults, deliberate confusion, or claims known to be false.
5. Distinguish attacks on the conclusion from attacks on its scope, assumptions, evidence, or wording.
6. Let the original position answer in its strongest form.
7. Permit partial revision. A narrower, more defensible belief may be a better outcome than total victory or defeat.
8. Preserve unresolved uncertainty rather than manufacturing closure.
9. End by recording confidence after and answering: **What changed?**

## Recommended flow

### 1. State the belief

Use one clear sentence.

### 2. Establish the starting model

Explain why the belief is held, which experiences or evidence support it, and what would count against it.

### 3. Present the strongest challenge

Construct a serious alternative model. Include the strongest evidence, logical objections, counterexamples, and consequences available.

### 4. Defend and revise

Respond directly. Concede valid points. Identify weak points. Narrow or reformulate the belief when justified.

### 5. Judge the result

Ask:

- Did the belief survive?
- Did its confidence change?
- Did its wording become more precise?
- Was a hidden assumption exposed?
- What evidence would settle the remaining dispute?

## Failure modes

- choosing a belief with no personal significance;
- arguing merely to win;
- confusing emotional discomfort with refutation;
- shifting definitions during the discussion;
- treating one counterexample as decisive when the claim is probabilistic;
- refusing to concede valid points;
- exaggerating certainty after an inconclusive result; and
- replacing the original belief with an easier one without acknowledging the change.

## Completion standard

A Convince Me session is complete when the strongest opposing case has been represented fairly, the original position has answered it, confidence has been reassessed, and the final artifact states what changed.

# Principle: Model Thinking

## A model is not reality

A model is a wager about how reality might work.

Every model begins with incomplete information. It connects observations into a possible explanation that can be tested, challenged, refined, or replaced.

The value of a model is not that it is immediately correct. Its value is that it gives inquiry a structure.

The absence of a model is not neutrality. Without a model, there may be no prediction to compare with reality, no direction for investigation, and no clear account of what evidence would matter.

Curiosity begins with:

> What if?

Understanding grows through:

> How could I know if I am wrong?

## Operating rules

- Build models from observations, questions, and explicit assumptions—not certainty.
- Treat every model as provisional.
- Separate the evidence from the explanation built around it.
- Distinguish internal coherence from external confirmation.
- Allow confidence to rise or fall when new evidence appears.
- Replace a weaker model without treating revision as failure.
- Preserve unanswered questions rather than filling gaps with false certainty.

## The Wonder Rule

Wonder is not the opposite of disciplined inquiry. It is often the beginning of it.

The Thinking Gym does not reject a model merely because it is unconventional. It also does not accept a model because it is vivid, meaningful, or internally consistent.

For every model, ask:

- What observations inspired it?
- Which parts are observed and which are inferred?
- What assumptions does it require?
- What predictions or expectations follow from it?
- What evidence would strengthen it?
- What evidence would weaken it?
- What alternative models explain the same observations?

## Closing principle

> It is better to explore a thoughtful model with humility than to stop wondering entirely.

Curiosity creates possibilities. Evidence determines which possibilities earn confidence.

# Principle: Separate the Limits of Reality from the Limits of Knowledge

## The map is not the territory

No person, institution, book, scientific model, or artificial intelligence possesses all available knowledge.

Every conclusion is constrained by the information available at the time it is made. Therefore, the Thinking Gym distinguishes between:

> This is not supported by the evidence currently available.

and:

> This cannot be true.

Those statements are not equivalent.

The first identifies a limit of present knowledge. The second makes a claim about reality itself.

## The knowledge boundary

When evaluating an idea, classify the uncertainty carefully:

- contradicted by strong evidence;
- unsupported by current evidence;
- physically possible but speculative;
- presently untestable;
- inconsistent with established logic or mathematics;
- or simply beyond the evaluator's current access and understanding.

A lack of evidence is not proof of impossibility.

Possibility alone is not evidence of truth.

Both principles must remain active at the same time.

## The role of artificial intelligence

Artificial intelligence is a reasoning tool, not an omniscient observer.

Its conclusions are limited by:

- the information and tools available to it;
- the quality, coverage, and framing of that information;
- gaps or distortions in training and retrieval;
- and the reliability of its own reasoning.

An AI should not silently convert its inability to verify a claim into a claim that reality excludes it.

When evaluating a speculative idea, it should state:

- what it can currently support;
- what it cannot verify;
- what remains unknown;
- which inferences are being made;
- and what evidence would alter the assessment.

## The role of the human

Humans also operate with incomplete knowledge. Experience, intuition, imagination, and pattern recognition can generate hypotheses that existing models have not considered.

Those hypotheses deserve examination rather than automatic acceptance or dismissal.

Confidence must still be earned through evidence, reasoning, comparison, and testing.

## The Thinking Gym standard

Before declaring an idea impossible, ask:

- Does it violate established logic, mathematics, or physical constraints?
- Does strong evidence directly contradict it?
- Is the issue merely that no supporting evidence is currently available?
- Is the evaluator missing relevant data or access?
- Could the claim be reformulated into something testable?

Criticism should be aimed at the actual weak point: evidence, inference, assumptions, testability, or contradiction.

## Closing principle

> Never confuse the limits of current evidence with the limits of reality.

Reality is under no obligation to fit the present map. The responsibility of the Thinking Gym is to improve the map without pretending that every blank area already contains an answer.
